Pronunciation scoring that runs on the device.

Pronunciada listens to someone reading a sentence and says how well each sound, word and sentence was pronounced, in ten languages. It is open source, works offline, and costs nothing per request.

What you get back

One call returns probabilities between 0 and 1 at three levels, with a strictness setting you can turn up or down.

Sounds, words, sentences

A calibrated probability for each, with start and end times, plus fluency and completeness for the sentence.

Mandarin tones

The tone expected and the tone heard for every syllable. Azure does not report tones.

Word stress

Whether the stress fell on the right syllable, in English, German, Spanish, Italian, Portuguese and Russian.

Length and hard contrasts

Japanese long and short vowels and doubled consonants, Korean and Mandarin aspiration, Russian soft and hard consonants.

Coaching output

For a flagged sound: what was expected, what it sounded like, which mouth feature differed, and a short tip your app can reword or translate.

Reading slips and recording checks

Skipped words, extra speech and long pauses, and a warning when the recording is clipped, too noisy or mostly silence.

Use it

A Python call, a command line, a local server that answers in Azure's response format, and a tool for AI agents.

import pronunciada

result = pronunciada.score("clip.wav", "The ball is in their court now", lang="en")

result.prob                        # 0.55  probability the sentence would be accepted
for word in result.words:
    print(word.text, word.prob)    # COURT 0.22
    for sound in word.phones:
        print(sound.ipa, sound.prob, sound.heard)

for item in result.coaching():    # expected sound, heard sound, what differed, a tip
    print(item)
# a readable report in the terminal
$ pronunciada check clip.wav "The ball is in their court now" --lang en --coach

# the full result as JSON, for pipelines
$ pronunciada check clip.wav "我家有四口人" --lang zh --json
# Already parsing Azure Pronunciation Assessment JSON? Keep your parser.
from pronunciada.compat.azure import to_azure
payload = to_azure(pronunciada.score("clip.wav", "Good morning", lang="en"))

# or run a local server that answers in the same shape
$ pronunciada serve --azure-compat --port 8765

# The format matches; the numbers are Pronunciada's own, so re-pick your thresholds.
# expose pronunciation scoring as a tool to Claude, Cursor and other MCP clients
$ pip install "pronunciada[mcp]"
$ pronunciada mcp

How it works

No large model was trained for this. It combines an open speech model with careful measurement.

An open sound recogniser

ZIPA turns audio into probabilities over the sounds of the world's languages. It never sees the expected text.

Evidence for each expected sound

The expected text becomes a sequence of sounds. For each one we measure the evidence that it was said, replaced or skipped, without forcing an alignment.

Calibrated probabilities

Small models trained on expert-scored learner speech turn the evidence into probabilities for sounds, words and sentences.

Small specialists

Tiny models read the recogniser's internal signals for what its output drops: tones, stress, length, aspiration. Everything trained for this project adds about 10 MB.

What it does not do yet

  • Proof on real learners exists for English and Mandarin only. The other eight languages are tested on native recordings and volunteer-reviewed clips, because no public learner data with expert marks exists for them.
  • English sentence intonation is behind Azure (0.77 against 0.83). Closing that gap took speech models too large for a phone.
  • Some contrasts are still missed: Italian double consonants, Portuguese nasal vowels, and Japanese pitch accent, which is not scored at all.
  • It needs the expected text. For free speech, pass in a transcript from your own speech recogniser.
  • It is a Python package today. It runs on laptops and servers; iOS, Android and browser builds are not written yet.

Why this exists

Almost every language app that grades pronunciation pays a cloud service for each recording and sends its learners' voices there. Pronunciada started at Moglearn, a language-learning app, as an attempt to do the same job on the learner's own device. The idea was to take an open speech model, add classical scoring methods and each language's sound rules, and improve it through a long series of measured rounds against the commercial service, with an AI coding agent doing the implementation. Every round was selected on development data and tested once on held-out data, and the rounds that failed are written up next to the ones that worked.
