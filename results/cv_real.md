# cv-real: real Common Voice speech judged by real people (ours vs Azure)

Generated by `python -m bench.eval_cv --steps report` (2026-11-22); Pronunciada package fingerprint `f7b0264ca55091df`. Test half only; the dev half (same size, disjoint speakers) is reserved for future tuning and has never been scored or sent to Azure.

Pre-specified before any result was seen: primary score ours = sentence acceptance probability (`SentenceResult.prob`, default strictness), Azure = `PronScore`; primary metric = AUC with 95 % bootstrap CIs resampling speakers and a paired speaker-bootstrap two-sided p-value for ours − Azure. Everything in the sensitivity table is secondary. Nothing in `pronunciada/` was tuned on this data.

Our numbers come from the Pronunciada tree at the fingerprint above; the English sentence scorer and the Mandarin tone model were being changed by other work at the time, so re-run `python -m bench.eval_cv --steps ours,report` before quoting them (Azure responses are cached and are not re-sent).

**How to read this.** Test A asks whether a system scores a recording lower when Common Voice
volunteers voted it down ("does this recording match the sentence?") than when they accepted it
unanimously. Reviewer rejections are not mispronunciation labels: they include misread or skipped
words, wrong sentences, truncated or noisy recordings, very strong accents, and reviewers' own
mistakes; acceptances can still contain mild errors. Volunteers are not trained raters and each
clip has only a handful of votes. A high AUC therefore means "agrees with crowd rejection", which
mixes pronunciation with reading accuracy and audio quality; neither system was built for exactly
this question, and absolute values are not comparable with the English expert-rated benchmarks.
Test A-weak (Korean only) adds clips with a single down-vote and no up-vote from
the not-yet-decided pool; one down-vote is a much weaker label than the strict rule (≥ 2 down-votes
and down − up ≥ 2): many such clips would have been accepted with more votes.
Test B compares accepted (correctly read) clips of speakers who self-declare a non-native accent
with speakers who declare a native variety. A pronunciation scorer is expected to give accented
speech somewhat lower scores, but a lower score here is **not** an error: the words were read
correctly and judged acceptable by reviewers, and accent labels are self-reported and sparse.
Both sides of every comparison are matched on prompt length and duration (stratified sampling);
the "length-only" rows show what prompt length or duration alone would achieve (≈ 0.5 means the
matching removed that shortcut).

## Data: source, cleaning and matching

Source for every locale: **Common Voice Corpus 22.0**, licence **CC0-1.0** (stated on the mirror's dataset card), Hugging Face mirror `fsicoli/common_voice_22_0` at revision `ae911de250fe9375d08e2daa2105b671d659d446`. Only the TSV metadata and the tar shards named below were read; shards were streamed and only selected clips were stored (16 kHz FLAC, outside the repo). Manifests (clip ids, pseudonymous speaker labels, votes, derived native / non-native flag, source shard): `bench/data/cv_real/<locale>.jsonl`; build log with all counts: `bench/data/cv_real/build.json`.

Rules: rejected: invalidated, down_votes >= 2 and up_votes <= down_votes - 2; accepted: dev/test (validated), up_votes >= 2 and down_votes == 0; duration: 1.0 s <= clip_durations <= 15.0 s; text: non-Latin-script locales (zh-CN, zh-TW, ja, ru, ko): no digits / Latin letters; silence: near-silent if < 0.3 s of 25 ms frames above -45.0 dBFS; per_speaker: 2; target_per_side_per_half: 200; seed: 20261116; strata: 5 character-length x 4 duration quantile bins of the positives.

Apart from ko, where Common Voice 22.0 has only 279 clearly rejected clips from 47 speakers in total, the 1–7 missing pairs are strata where the near-silent rule removed more clips than the fetched reserve (35 % + 2 per stratum); a stratum is always trimmed on both sides so the matching stays exact.
