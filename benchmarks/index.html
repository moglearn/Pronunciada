Benchmarks · November 23, 2026

Benchmarks on real learner speech

Pronunciada, OpenPronounce, Azure Pronunciation Assessment, Lingolix and Chivox scored the same recordings of language learners in English, Mandarin, Spanish and German. No synthetic speech is used on this page.

The comparison covers complete pronunciation-assessment systems: a library, app or API that takes a recording and the text that was read, and returns scores for sounds, words and the sentence. OpenPronounce is the other open-source system of that kind. Azure, Lingolix and Chivox are commercial cloud services. Every system received the same audio and text, and none was tuned on these recordings.

Accuracy and size

A system that runs on the learner's device has to be downloaded and held in memory, so its size matters as much as its scores. The chart places each system by the size of its model files and by its score on four English tests that every system could take.

Pronunciada loads 107 MB of files with its int8 model and 297 MB with the full model. OpenPronounce loads 396 MB with its distilled English model and 2,603 MB in default mode.

On the speechocean762 test set, Pronunciada agrees with the experts most closely on sounds (0.700) and on words (0.640), and its difference to each other system on those two measures is significant. On sentence scores, Pronunciada (0.792), OpenPronounce (0.790, and 0.802 distilled) and Azure (0.796) are not significantly different. OpenPronounce is significantly better at finding the sounds annotators marked as mispronounced: 0.743 and 0.753 against 0.712 on L2-ARCTIC, and 0.741 and 0.739 against 0.721 on EpaDB.

What these tests do not show

  • The kind of label differs by language. Expert or teacher scores exist for English and Mandarin, and marked sounds for English only. Spanish and German have only the distinction between learners and native speakers, which rewards anything that differs between the two groups, speaking rate included.
  • Several sets have few speakers: 6 in L2-ARCTIC, 20 in EpaDB, 18 learners in OMPAL, 10 learners and 8 native speakers in Heroico, 7 native speakers in LeaP German. One speaker can move such a figure by several points.
  • There is no learner data here for Japanese, Korean, Russian, Portuguese, French or Italian. These tests say nothing about those languages.
  • The commercial systems are incomplete. Azure was not run on German, Lingolix was run on speechocean762 and Heroico only, and Chivox on a sample of two sets. No other commercial service was run.
  • Pronunciada and OpenPronounce were both fitted on the training part of speechocean762. Neither used L2-ARCTIC, EpaDB, Heroico, the Speech Accent Archive or LeaP.
  • Disk, memory and time were measured on one laptop. Nothing was measured on a phone.

Method

Versions: Pronunciada from the main branch of its repository, with the full model and the int8 model. OpenPronounce 1.1.0, the latest release on the day of the runs, in default mode and with its distilled English phone model. Azure Pronunciation Assessment through its REST API, region westus, November 2026. Lingolix API, model 2026-11. Chivox speech evaluation API, sentence engines for English and Mandarin. ZIPA alone is zipa-small-crctc-500k with the standard goodness-of-pronunciation score.

Every interval and every difference comes from 10,000 resamples of speakers, with the systems compared on the same resamples. A difference is called significant at p < 0.05, without correction for the number of comparisons. Disk, memory and time were measured on an Apple M3 Pro with no GPU.

The full tables on GitHub have every row with its interval, the rank-correlation versions of the tests, the construction of the German set and the commands to reproduce the page.
